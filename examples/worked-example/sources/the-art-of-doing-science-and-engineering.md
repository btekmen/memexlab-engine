# The Art of Doing Science and Engineering

Richard Hamming's Bell Labs / Stanford lectures on how great scientists think, learn, and choose
problems — an intellectual playbook for producing significant work.

## Key ideas (paraphrased, for extraction)

- Work on important problems — most people never seriously ask which problems matter.
- "Luck favors the prepared mind": chance × preparation = discovery.
- Learning to learn is the meta-skill; education is building better mental models, not accumulating facts.
- Intellectual courage and independent thinking are strategic advantages; experts can become prisoners of accepted ideas.
- Knowledge compounds like capital — small improvements to thinking, methods, and tools accumulate.

Hamming's framing: "The purpose of computing is insight, not numbers."
