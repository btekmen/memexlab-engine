# The compounding stack — abstraction, maintenance, judgment

A `memex-frameworks` pass *across* four sources, asking what actually makes knowledge **compound**.

- **Abstraction** ([[abstraction-creates-scale]], Hopper) — make complexity accessible and a whole layer becomes buildable on top.
- **Maintenance** ([[the-memex]], [[associative-trails]], Bush) — a curated, agent-maintained store with associative trails keeps that layer usable as it grows.
- **Judgment** ([[work-on-important-problems]], [[luck-favors-the-prepared-mind]], Hamming) — the scarce input is choosing *what* to compound: preparation and good questions.
- **Execution** ([[small-autonomous-teams]], [[speed-as-strategy]], Skunk Works) — small, fast, autonomous loops turn the above into shipped results.

## First principles

Strip these to one claim: in the AI era the bottleneck is not access to information but
**judgment about what's worth compounding**. MemexLab's job is to make abstraction + maintenance
reliable so scarce human attention goes to judgment.

## Second-order

As extraction and retrieval get cheap, the differentiator shifts to the *frameworks and
questions* applied to the corpus — which is why `memex-frameworks` and `memex-progress` matter
more than raw ingestion. (This echoes the harness thesis: compounding comes from the
scaffolding, not the model.)
